Pullback Watch

Tracks one rule on eight broad US index funds: after a sharp five-day fall in a fund that is still in an uptrend, buy at the next open and sell five sessions later.

Read this first. The rule is not confirmed. On data it had never seen (2020–2026) it won 66.8% of the time against 55.9% for the same funds on any day, in line with its training years, but it missed the pre-set statistical bar and had two poor years. This page exists to collect new evidence. It is a research tracker, not a recommendation.

{# ── The rule ── #}
The rule
Signal, at the close: the fund is down {{ '%.0f' | format(-(rule.drop_pct if rule.drop_pct is defined else -3)) }}% or more over the last {{ rule.lookback if rule.lookback is defined else 5 }} sessions and its close is above its {{ rule.sma if rule.sma is defined else 200 }}-session average.
Buy at the next session's open. Sell at the close {{ rule.hold if rule.hold is defined else 5 }} sessions after the signal. No stop, no target.
One position per fund: a fund being held cannot signal again until it is sold. Each of the eight funds is tracked on its own here; the back-tested portfolio held at most five at a time, 20% each.
Funds: SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA, MDY, IJR, VTI, RSP. They move together, so several signals on one day are one market pullback, not several independent bets.

  • It is a small earner. About 11 separate market pullbacks a year; cash more than 90% of the time. Over the holdout years SPY held throughout made {{ bt.holdout.spy_cagr }}% a year with a {{ bt.holdout.spy_dd }}% drawdown.
  • Its worst trades are the start of a crash. The entries of 24 February and 2 March 2020 lost 7–12% before prices fell through the 200-day average and switched the rule off.
  • It is sensitive to the definition. The same idea triggered by a 20-day band instead of a five-day fall looked second best on the training years and failed on the holdout (54% against 56%). Without the 200-day condition the lift shrinks to under 4 points.
  • Individual stocks showed nothing. The same search across about 8,400 listed and delisted stocks found no condition that picks winners after costs.
