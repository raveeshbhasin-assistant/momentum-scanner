## The bet (in one sentence)

**The durable profit from AI in healthcare accrues not to the pre-revenue "AI drug discovery" story stocks the market keeps funding, but to the workflow and diagnostics incumbents that already own the systems of record — the EHR-adjacent platforms, imaging and diagnostics instrument bases, and molecular-data owners — because AI is a margin-and-throughput upgrade to their existing distribution, not a greenfield business someone else gets to build.**

## Time Horizon & Strategy

**Time Horizon:** 3-5 years.

**Strategy:** Long-only, quality-weighted. I want companies where AI is deflating cost or expanding volume *inside a business that already prints revenue* — the picks-and-shovels and the installed base — rather than companies whose entire equity value is a bet that an unproven AI pipeline eventually clears the FDA. I size heaviest on the workflow/data incumbents, hold the imaging and diagnostics instrument owners as the core, and carry only a small, explicitly speculative sleeve in AI-native drug discovery as optionality.

## The Future I'm Betting On

Healthcare is the largest information-processing industry on earth that still runs on manual labor: a doctor dictating notes, a coder translating those notes into billing, a radiologist eyeballing a scan, a lab tech interpreting a molecular result. AI does not need to *cure* anything to be transformational here — it needs to automate the clerical and interpretive layers that sit on top of workflows we already pay for. That is exactly the kind of AI that ships today and gets reimbursed.

The winners, I believe, are the companies that own the system of record the AI plugs into. An ambient scribe is only valuable if it writes into the EHR the hospital already runs. An imaging algorithm is only deployable if it runs on the scanner and PACS the hospital already bought. A diagnostic AI is only trusted if it is validated against the molecular database someone already spent a decade assembling. Incumbency — the installed base, the regulatory clearances, the data moat, the reimbursement relationships — is the bottleneck, and it is not for sale. The AI is the commodity layer; the distribution is the scarce asset.

## Why Now (The Catalyst)

Three things flipped in 2025-2026. First, FDA AI-device clearances crossed ~1,450 cumulative by end-2025, with radiology taking ~75% of the flow and GE HealthCare, Siemens, and Philips leading the manufacturer tables — regulatory throughput is no longer the gate. Second, ambient AI documentation went from pilot to standard of care: 150+ health systems deploying Nuance/Microsoft DAX Copilot inside Epic, Abridge at a ~$5.3B valuation, Doximity reporting 300,000+ unique clinicians using its AI tools in a single quarter. Third, the AI-native diagnostics companies posted real, large revenue — Tempus grew 2025 revenue 83% to $1.27B; Natera and Guardant are compounding 20-40% on actual test volume. The theme stopped being a slide and started being an income statement.

## Vehicles Risk

The central risk is *"AI is real, but it's a feature, not a moat."* If AI documentation, imaging triage, and coding automation become table-stakes commodities that every vendor ships and no vendor can charge a premium for, then the incumbents get the productivity but not the pricing power, and the upside compresses into the diversified mega-cap lines (see: exclusions). This is the mirror image of the GLP-1 "thesis right, vehicles wrong" problem, and I treat it as the primary risk, not a tail.

## What I Expect the Market to Underestimate

- **The data moat compounds silently.** Tempus, Natera, and Guardant are not "AI companies" in the narrative sense — they are multimodal molecular-data owners whose models get better as the installed test base grows. The market prices them as diagnostics volume stories and underrates the widening, un-replicable training corpus underneath.

- **Workflow AI is a margin event for incumbents, not a revenue event for startups.** The value of ambient documentation and coding automation mostly accrues to whoever owns the EHR/CRM system of record (and the physician distribution) — Veeva, Doximity, IQVIA — because that is where the AI is forced to live. Startups rent that distribution; they don't own it.

## What I Expect the Market to Over-Hype

- **Pre-revenue AI drug discovery platforms.** Multiple "AI-generated" drug candidates missed clinical endpoints in 2025. Recursion and Schrödinger remain deeply loss-making with pushed-out readouts. The market keeps paying platform multiples for what is still a binary, decade-long clinical bet. I hold these only as small optionality, never as core.

- **"AI diagnostic accuracy" narrative names with no reimbursement path.** A cleared algorithm that no payer pays for is a science project. I discount clearance counts and prize CPT/coverage.

## Supply Chain & Second-Order Summary

Weighted toward incumbency and real revenue:

1. **Clinical AI Workflow — Systems of Record (High Priority — Long):** Veeva (VEEV), Doximity (DOCS), IQVIA (IQV). The distribution layer AI is forced to run inside.

2. **Medical Imaging & Diagnostics AI (High Priority — Long):** GE HealthCare (GEHC), Philips (PHG), Intuitive Surgical (ISRG), IDEXX (IDXX). The installed instrument base and the FDA-clearance leaders.

3. **Healthcare Data & Diagnostics Infrastructure (High Priority — Long):** Tempus (TEM), Guardant (GH), Natera (NTRA), Exact Sciences (EXAS), Quest (DGX). The molecular-data owners and lab scale.

4. **AI Drug Discovery & Research Tools (Medium/Speculative — Long, small):** Thermo Fisher (TMO), Agilent (A) as real-revenue picks-and-shovels; Schrödinger (SDGR) and Recursion (RXRX) as labeled optionality only.

## What Would Make Me Wrong

- **Commoditization of workflow AI:** Two or more of Veeva/Doximity/IQVIA report that AI features are being given away to defend seats rather than monetized as upsell — signaling AI is dilutive to pricing power, not accretive.

- **Reimbursement reversal on AI diagnostics:** CMS or major private payers pull or refuse coverage/CPT reimbursement for AI-augmented diagnostics or MRD testing, collapsing the volume-to-revenue bridge for TEM/GH/NTRA/EXAS.

- **The data moat proves fake:** An open-source or hyperscaler foundation model (or a pooled consortium dataset) matches the proprietary molecular databases at Tempus/Natera on clinical benchmarks, erasing the "un-replicable corpus" premise underneath Bucket 3.

## Re-Check Triggers

I revisit this thesis if:

- **(Downside)** FDA AI-device clearance flow to the incumbent imaging trio (GEHC/PHG/Siemens) stalls or reverses for two consecutive quarters, or a major payer publicly rescinds AI-diagnostic reimbursement.

- **(Downside)** Epic (or another EHR owner) launches first-party ambient/coding AI that structurally undercuts the independent workflow names — I already see Epic entering the scribe market in 2025; if it takes >30% share I must re-weight away from workflow renters.

- **(Upside)** A pre-revenue AI-discovery name in the optionality sleeve posts an unambiguous, AI-attributable Phase 2/3 clinical win — this would force me to revisit whether the discovery bucket deserves a larger, non-speculative allocation.
