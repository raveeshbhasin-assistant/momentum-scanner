## The bet (in one sentence)

**The enablers of the US factory build-out — electrical/mechanical E&C contractors, factory automation, aggregates/building products, and the industrial REITs that landlord the footprint — capture durable, backlog-visible value from the largest US manufacturing-construction cycle since the 1970s, while the end-manufacturers themselves stay too diffuse and competitive to own.**

---

## Time Horizon & Strategy

**Time Horizon:** 3-5 years for the construction super-cycle to convert from backlog into installed capacity, with an 18-24 month backlog-visibility catalyst as CHIPS/IRA-funded megaprojects break ground and contractor books-to-bill stay above 1.

**Strategy:** Anchor on names with multi-year backlog and structural pricing power (aggregates local-monopolies, specialty E&C with negotiated work, infill REITs). Harvest into narrative spikes on the higher-beta contractors; hold the materials + REIT names as long-cycle compounders through the whole build.

## The Future I'm Betting On

Over the next 3 to 5 years the United States executes the largest manufacturing-construction cycle since the 1970s. Real construction spending on manufacturing structures — a hard, government-measured series — has roughly tripled off its pre-2021 base, driven by three overlapping policy engines: the CHIPS Act (semiconductor fabs), the Inflation Reduction Act (batteries, solar, EV, clean-energy manufacturing), and a tariff regime that raises the cost of importing finished goods and pulls production onshore. This is not a forecast; the concrete is already being poured.

The future I am betting on is that value accrues to the **enablers of the build-out, not the end-manufacturers**. Building a chip fab or a battery gigafactory requires enormous quantities of the same boring inputs: electrical and mechanical construction labor, switchgear and process piping, factory automation and control systems, aggregates and cement for the slabs and roads, and warehouse/logistics space to move materials in and product out. These suppliers have backlog visibility measured in years, negotiated (not bid-to-the-bone) pricing, and — in the case of aggregates and infill industrial land — genuine local-monopoly economics. The end-manufacturers (auto OEMs, generic contract fabricators, commodity solar) face brutal competition, overcapacity risk, and diffuse returns. I want to own the toll-booths on the build, not the factories themselves.

## Why Now (The Catalyst)

The catalyst is that the policy money has moved from *authorization* to *ground-breaking*. CHIPS and IRA grants and loan guarantees have been obligated; the megaprojects (Arizona, Texas, Ohio, the battery belt across the Southeast) are in active construction, and the second wave — supplier plants, upstream chemicals, grid interconnects — is queuing behind them. Contractor backlogs are at record levels with books-to-bill above 1, meaning demand is being *added* faster than it is being burned. Simultaneously the tariff regime has hardened into a durable feature of trade policy rather than a negotiating tactic, which changes multi-decade site-location math for corporate capex committees right now. The enabler cohort has order books that already price in 2027-2028 revenue.

## Vehicles Risk

Unlike a pure-play theme (e.g. space), reshoring's public-market expression is **broad and liquid** — this is the rare thesis where the vehicles are mostly large, profitable, US-listed compounders. The risk is not "thesis right, vehicles wrong"; it is **crowding and valuation**. Several of these names (STRL, EME, aggregates) have already re-rated hard on the reshoring narrative, so entry multiple discipline matters more than vehicle scarcity. A second risk is **overlap**: ROK, ETN, and ETN-adjacent electrical names also appear in the AI-Data-Center and Robotics themes; to keep reshoring differentiated I lean into the construction + materials + REIT breadth that is *unique* to this build-out and treat the automation names as the smaller, shared sleeve.

## What I Expect the Market to Underestimate

- **The durability of aggregates pricing power.** The market models Vulcan and Martin Marietta as cyclical building-materials stocks. In reality, crushed stone is a local-monopoly business — it is uneconomic to truck aggregates more than ~50 miles, so each quarry is a regional toll-booth with structural, above-inflation pricing that compounds through a multi-year volume cycle. This is the single most under-appreciated pricing-power story in the cohort.

- **Backlog visibility as a de-risker.** Specialty E&C contractors (PWR, EME, FIX, STRL) carry multi-year negotiated backlogs that the market still prices with a cyclical-contractor discount. As books-to-bill stays above 1 and megaproject work converts to revenue, the earnings become far more visible and less cyclical than the historical multiple implies.

- **Industrial REIT re-leasing spreads.** Infill industrial land (REXR's Southern California, PLD's logistics nodes) is effectively un-reproducible near population centers. As leases roll to market, the mark-to-market re-leasing spreads are larger and more durable than a rate-sensitive REIT screen suggests.

## What I Expect the Market to Over-Hype

- **The end-manufacturers themselves.** Auto OEMs, generic solar/battery cell makers, and commodity contract fabricators get the reshoring headlines but face overcapacity, subsidy-cliff risk, and Chinese competition. Their returns on the reshoring capex are diffuse — they are the *cost*, not the beneficiary. Deliberately excluded.

- **Pure "American manufacturing" thematic ETFs and story stocks.** Narrative-driven baskets that stuff in any US-domiciled manufacturer dilute the actual bottleneck exposure. The value is concentrated in a handful of enabler sub-sectors, not in "made in USA" as a label.

- **The most re-rated contractors at peak multiple.** STRL and EME have run hard; the market may extrapolate current margins and book-to-bill indefinitely. I want these names but at a disciplined entry, and I flag the risk that a construction-cycle wobble compresses their premium multiples fastest.

## Supply Chain Summary

Sourcing prioritizes backlog visibility + pricing power over raw growth, across four buckets:

1. **E&C / Contractors (High Priority):** Electrical + mechanical + site-development construction firms that physically build the factories, grid tie-ins, and pads. Backlog visibility is the key screen. (PWR, EME, FIX, MTZ, PRIM, STRL)

2. **Factory Automation & Capital Goods (High Priority):** Automation, control, power-management, and motion/capital-goods suppliers that monetize plants as they come online and equip. Some overlap with AI-DC/Robotics — treated as the shared sleeve. (ROK, EMR, ETN, CMI, PH)

3. **Building Products / Aggregates / Materials (High Priority):** Local-monopoly aggregates + diversified materials + building-products distribution. The purest pricing-power layer. (VMC, MLM, CRH, BLDR)

4. **Industrial / Logistics REITs (Medium Priority):** The landlords of the reshored logistics + light-manufacturing footprint. Rate-sensitive, so sized as the medium-priority yield sleeve. (PLD, REXR, STAG)

## What Would Make Me Wrong

- **Policy reversal or subsidy clawback.** A change in administration or Congress that repeals, defunds, or claws back CHIPS/IRA obligations before the second wave breaks ground would collapse the forward backlog. This is the primary falsifier.

- **Tariff regime unwinds.** If tariffs are negotiated away and the multi-decade site-location math flips back toward offshoring, the *marginal* reshoring decision reverses and megaproject pipelines thin out.

- **Megaproject cancellations cascade.** If two or more flagship fab/gigafactory projects are canceled or indefinitely paused (demand shortfall, cost overruns, chip/EV glut), contractor backlogs deflate and the aggregates volume cycle rolls over.

- **A construction recession from rates.** A sustained high-rate environment that freezes private non-residential construction and REIT cap rates would hit the whole cohort at once — E&C backlog cancellations, aggregates volume decline, REIT NAV compression.

- **The enablers get bid away.** If the cohort re-rates so far that entry multiples fully discount the 2027-2028 backlog, the thesis is directionally right but the equity upside is already priced — a valuation, not a direction, failure.

## Re-Check Triggers

I revisit this thesis if any of the falsifiers above appear, or if:

- **(Downside)** Aggregate US construction spending on manufacturing structures (Census series) turns negative year-over-year for two consecutive quarters.

- **(Downside)** The top contractor names (PWR, EME, STRL) report book-to-bill below 1.0 for two consecutive quarters, signaling backlog burn is outpacing new awards.

- **(Downside)** Aggregates pricing (VMC/MLM average selling price) decelerates below the rate of input-cost inflation, signaling the pricing-power thesis is breaking.

- **(Downside)** Industrial REIT (PLD/REXR) same-store re-leasing spreads compress toward zero, signaling the infill land-scarcity premium is fading.

- **(Upside)** CHIPS/IRA second-wave obligations (supplier plants, upstream chemicals, grid) exceed the first-wave dollar total, or a new industrial-policy package is enacted — signaling the build-out extends beyond the current 3-5 year window. Triggers larger position sizes on the highest-backlog-visibility E&C and aggregates names.
