## The Future I'm Betting On

Over the next 3 to 5 years, the space industry will shift from a "launch-constrained" market to a "hardware- and component-constrained" market. For the last decade, the primary barrier to space was the exorbitant cost and scarcity of rocket launches. With next-generation heavy-lift vehicles like SpaceX's Starship achieving operational status over the next 12 to 24 months, the cost to deliver mass to Low Earth Orbit (LEO) is collapsing. This will trigger a massive infrastructure buildout.

This is structurally a defense-anchored bet (70% weight on Space Force / NRO / proliferated LEO procurement programs), with 30% on commercial infrastructure that benefits from the same launch deflation. The U.S. military is urgently pivoting toward "proliferated" architectures — replacing a few billion-dollar satellites with interconnected networks of thousands of cheaper small satellites.

The future I am betting on is that the market is looking at the wrong bottlenecks. Cheap launch capacity means an exponential increase in the volume of hardware that must be built, powered, and connected in orbit. The real value over the next 3 to 5 years will not be captured by the headline-grabbing launch providers or speculative space applications. Instead, it will flow to the boring, unglamorous suppliers of mission-critical subsystems — such as radiation-hardened microchips, satellite buses, optical communication links, and advanced propulsion. By focusing on companies with real revenue and deep backlogs today, we can capture the structural upside of this orbital buildout while avoiding the highly dilutive, pre-revenue risks of the sector.

## Why Now (The Catalyst)

We are seeing a perfect convergence of technological execution and geopolitical necessity. SpaceX's heavy-lift program is reaching a flight cadence that fundamentally resets the economics of space access. Simultaneously, rising geopolitical tensions have forced the U.S. Department of Defense and the Space Force to aggressively accelerate funding for resilient, distributed satellite constellations. The contract flow has turned from a trickle of experimental awards into multi-billion dollar baseline procurement programs, establishing a highly capitalized government "anchor tenant" for the space supply chain right now.

## Vehicles Risk

Unlike AI Data Center, the public-market expression of this thesis is structurally narrow. SpaceX — the dominant beneficiary of the entire supply chain — is private and likely stays that way. Many launch-adjacent companies came public via SPAC and carry dilution problems that the capital structure criterion will heavily penalize. We expect the candidate universe to be 15-20 names total (vs. 28 for AI DC), with the final 5-7 picks weighted toward names with real revenue today rather than projections. **The "thesis right, vehicles wrong" risk is the central risk for this theme, not a tail risk.**

## Vehicles Scope: Global by Necessity

Because the U.S. public-market pool is shallow and SPAC-polluted, our search cannot be bound by geography. European defense and aerospace primes (such as Airbus and Thales) alongside specialized Japanese and Israeli component and subsystem providers via ADRs are part of the candidate universe by design, not as an afterthought. This global scope ensures we find pure-play engineering exposure without compromising on balance sheet health.

## What I Expect the Market to Underestimate

- **The Moat of Specialty Space Components:** The market treats satellite manufacturing like consumer electronics. In reality, building hardware that can survive severe radiation, extreme thermal cycles, and zero-gravity requires specialized engineering. Companies that own the intellectual property for radiation-hardened electronics, precise star trackers, and satellite power management hold immense pricing power over prime contractors.

- **Optical and Inter-Satellite Laser Communications:** As thousands of satellites enter orbit, radio frequency spectrum will hit strict capacity limits and regulatory hurdles. The market underestimates the absolute necessity of optical laser communication links to allow satellites to route data to each other instantly in space without relying constantly on ground stations.

## What I Expect the Market to Over-Hype

- **Pure-Play Commercial Launch Competitors:** The market continuously funds early-stage rocket startups hoping to find "the next SpaceX." Competing with established reusability and unmatched unit economics is a capital-intensive race to the bottom. Most public launch pure-plays will remain cash-burning vehicles.

- **Unprofitable Downstream Software and Pure Commercial Analytics:** Companies claiming they will revolutionize agriculture, climate tracking, or finance using proprietary satellite data imagery are fighting an increasingly commoditized market. Without a highly capitalized government backer, these business models suffer from long sales cycles and poor capital efficiency.

- **Mega-Constellation Telecom Operators (D2D + Consumer Broadband):** The market loves the recurring-revenue framing of direct-to-device satellite broadband and consumer LEO comms. But the unit economics on D2D are unproven, the capital intensity is brutal, and competition from terrestrial 5G plus Starlink (which dominates without being publicly investable) caps the margin opportunity. AST SpaceMobile, the D2D side of Iridium, and other pure-play comms operators face years of dilution before sustainable cash flow.

## Supply Chain Summary

To reflect our 70/30 defense weighting and the "real revenue today" mandate, candidate sourcing and allocation sizing will prioritize the upstream infrastructure layers over speculative downstream applications:

1. **Mission-Critical Subsystems & Components (High Priority):** Radiation-hardened semiconductors, telemetry subsystems, high-efficiency solar cells, and advanced aerospace components.

2. **Space Communications & Ground Infrastructure (High Priority):** Inter-satellite optical laser links, phased-array antennas, and dedicated orbit-to-ground communications infrastructure networks.

3. **Payload Data & Defense Analytics (Medium Priority):** High-margin tactical geospatial AI, intelligence processing software, and Earth observation analytics backed by recurring government contracts.

4. **In-Space Logistics & Satellite Architecture (Low/Speculative Priority):** Satellite buses, orbital transfer vehicles, space domain awareness tracking, and in-orbit refueling mechanisms. **Names from this bucket must clear a higher bar on capital structure quality and real revenue today to be promoted to the tracker — long monetization timelines mean we're betting on optionality, not cash flow.**

## What Would Make Me Wrong

- **Heavy-Lift Reusability Stalls or Regulates Out:** If major next-gen heavy-lift vehicles suffer catastrophic operational failures or strict environmental/regulatory bans that freeze launch cadences, the cost of space access remains high, choking off downstream infrastructure demand.

- **Sustained U.S. Government Budget Disruption:** Any prolonged systemic budget shock — such as long-term federal government shutdowns, sequestration, or a paralyzing continuing resolution standoff — that delays procurement and contract deployment across the Space Force, NRO, and NASA cohorts simultaneously.

- **Antitrust and Consolidation Caps:** If two or more of our top 5 tracker names get acquired by tier-1 defense primes at premiums below 25% — signaling primes are absorbing value before public investors capture the upside — the thesis "wins" on direction but public equity returns get prematurely capped.

- **Ground Station Commoditization:** Tech giants or hyperscalers successfully scale automated "Ground-Station-as-a-Service" models (AWS Ground Station and equivalents) so efficiently that it destroys the profit margins and infrastructure value of standalone space communications networks.

- **Thesis is Right, But the Vehicles Are Wrong:** The space economy enters a golden era, but the entire financial upside is captured privately by SpaceX or absorbed seamlessly into the massive, diversified revenue lines of mega-cap defense conglomerates, leaving public pure-play space equities with terrible, dilutive returns.

## Re-Check Triggers

I revisit this thesis if any of the falsifiers above appear, or if:

- **(Downside)** Space Force or Space Development Agency (SDA) funding for proliferated LEO architectures gets cut or delayed by more than 15% in a federal budget cycle.

- **(Downside)** Next-generation heavy-lift commercial launch costs fail to drop below $1,000/kg on an operational basis by the end of 2027.

- **(Downside)** Major payload integrators report sequential margin compression, signaling that hyperscalers or government buyers are successfully squeezing component supplier pricing power.

- **(Upside)** Proliferated LEO contract awards exceed $5B in a single quarter from any combination of Space Force, SDA, and NRO — signaling the procurement program is accelerating beyond plan. Triggers a rotation from momentum names into long-cycle infrastructure suppliers, with larger position sizes on top tracker names.
