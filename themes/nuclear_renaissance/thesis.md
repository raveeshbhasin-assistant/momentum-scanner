## The Future I'm Betting On

For thirty years, nuclear was a melting ice cube: shrinking Western capacity, a fuel cycle hollowed out by cheap Russian SWU and Kazakh pounds, zero new demand. That regime ended in roughly eighteen months. Data-center load growth gave the grid its first real demand shock in a generation, and hyperscalers — the most creditworthy buyers on earth — started signing 17-to-20-year nuclear PPAs: Microsoft/Constellation at Crane (837 MW), Amazon/Talen at Susquehanna (1.92 GW), Meta/Constellation at Clinton (1.1 GW), Google/Kairos for 500 MW of SMRs. Over 9.8 GW of nuclear capacity is now committed to AI infrastructure. Washington ratified the shift: the May 2025 executive orders target 400 GW of US nuclear capacity by 2050, NRC licensing reform, and a DOE pilot program pushing advanced reactors toward criticality.

The future I'm betting on is one where demand is no longer the question — supply is. Palisades is about to become the first decommissioned US reactor ever returned to service; Crane restarts in 2027. Every megawatt needs uranium (~180 Mlb/yr of reactor demand against ~130 Mlb of primary mine supply), conversion, and enrichment — a stack the West spent two decades ceding to Russia and Kazakhstan. Rebuilding it is slow, licensed, and bottlenecked. That is where the durable pricing power lives.

## Why Now (The Catalyst)

Three catalysts stack in the next 18 months. First, restarts go from press release to grid-synced electrons: Palisades in 2026, Crane in 2027 — proof that "nuclear can execute" flips the sector narrative. Second, the fuel cycle just got its anchor contract: Centrus signed a $900M fixed-price DOE contract (June 30, 2026) for the first new US-owned commercial HALEU enrichment capacity at Piketon, atop a $2.4B LEU backlog. Third, supply keeps disappointing on cue — Kazatomprom guided 2026 output down, Cameco cut McArthur River guidance — while the Russian-supply ban forces Western utilities to recontract into a market with no slack.

## Vehicles Risk

Many headline winners are uninvestable: Kazatomprom lists in London/Astana, Urenco and Orano are state-owned, Holtec, TerraPower, X-energy, and Kairos are private, and Westinghouse sits inside Cameco (49%) and Brookfield. The investable pure-plays skew toward pre-revenue SMR stories trading on announcements. The risk: the renaissance happens but value pools in private and sovereign hands while public vehicles dilute. I manage this by anchoring the book in names with revenue and backlog today (CCJ, BWXT, LEU, CEG, MIR, CW) and capping the speculative sleeve.

## Vehicles Scope: US-Listed, Fidelity-Holdable Only

All candidates are US-listed common stock or liquid sponsored listings (the Canadian miners — CCJ, NXE — trade as full NYSE listings). No unsponsored OTC, no local-only foreign listings; Kazatomprom is excluded on exactly those grounds, the way robotics excluded Keyence.

## What I Expect the Market to Underestimate

- **Enrichment and conversion scarcity.** The market prices miners off the spot pound but treats SWU and conversion as plumbing. There is exactly one US-owned enricher (Centrus), Western conversion runs flat out, and HALEU — required by most advanced designs — barely exists outside Russia. The 5-of-5 bottleneck.
- **The existing fleet's cash flows.** Uprates, extensions, and restarts are the cheapest megawatts in the buildout; unregulated nuclear fleets (CEG, TLN) are scarce, non-replicable assets hyperscalers are bidding for years ahead of any SMR shipping.

## What I Expect the Market to Over-Hype

- **Pre-revenue SMR developers.** OKLO and NuScale trade on multi-gigawatt "programs" and MOUs years before first revenue. Timelines will slip, costs will surprise (Vogtle is the base rate), dilution is certain. I hold them small for optionality, not conviction.
- **Every ISR junior with a claim map.** Sub-scale miners restarting high-cost pounds get bid as if $85/lb is a floor forever. Cost curves and permitting still matter.

## What Would Make Me Wrong

- **The AI demand anchor breaks:** two or more of the announced hyperscaler nuclear PPAs (Crane, Susquehanna, Clinton, Kairos) are cancelled or materially repriced down as data-center capex slows — removing the creditworthy buyer that underwrites the entire buildout.
- **A supply flood:** Kazatomprom reverses its cuts, Rook I and other tier-1 projects get sanctioned and fast-tracked, and uranium spot sustains below $60/lb for two consecutive quarters — proving the deficit was a narrative, not a structure.
- **Execution failure re-poisons the well:** Palisades fails to sustain commercial operation in 2026, Crane slips past 2028, or the DOE pilot reactors miss criticality with Vogtle-style overruns — reviving "nuclear can't build" and freezing new orders.
