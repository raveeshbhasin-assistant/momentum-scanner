## The future I'm betting on

The market is obsessed with bipedal humanoids, but the real financial windfall of "embodied AI"
over the next five years will be captured by established industrial and logistics automation
companies whose existing installed hardware gets radically upgraded by AI vision models — alongside
the suppliers of the high-moat machine vision and sensors that make it possible.

The developed world is running out of working-age population. Labor substitution is no longer a
cost-saving luxury; it is an existential requirement for logistics and manufacturing. Recent
breakthroughs in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models mean robots no longer need to be rigidly
pre-programmed for a single repetitive task — they can "see" and adapt to unstructured environments.
The bet is that this intelligence upgrade drastically expands the addressable market for *existing*
robotic form factors (robotic arms, AGVs, smart sorting) long before bipedal robots are economically
viable on a factory floor.

Strategy is an **80/20 structural split**: 80% to real-revenue, mature automation and logistics
integrators as a 2–5 year structural hold driven by demographic labor shortages; 20% to high-moat
component suppliers (machine vision, tactile sensors) that provide optionality on the eventual
